Overview

AetherFlow is a formal architecture that replaces traditional merge queues with lock-free direct entry, backed by probabilistic post-merge verification and proactive auto-remediation agents. By decoupling validation from code admission, AetherFlow eliminates queue starvation and reduces integration latency by over an order of magnitude.

Formal Definitions

Definition 1 — Dynamic Conflict Window. Instead of blocking concurrent writes, AetherFlow evaluates the probability of semantic and structural conflict between parallel changesets cᵢ and cⱼ using a dynamic conflict window function:

W(cᵢ, cⱼ) = 1 − exp(−κ·O·e^(−τ/θ))

Where τ(cᵢ, cⱼ) is the temporal distance between changeset injections; O ∈ [0, 1] is the overlap score from file-footprint overlap and dependency-graph proximity; κ > 0 is the sensitivity; and θ > 0 is the decay horizon.

Proposition 2 — Monotonicity. Under Equation 1a, W is strictly decreasing in τ for O > 0, and strictly increasing in O for finite τ.

Definition 2 — Post-Merge Verification Operator. Changesets bypass pre-merge queues and commit directly to the primary reference log. Asynchronous verification is computed recursively via the verification operator ⊕_V:

V(c) = ⊕_V(δ_i, {V(p) | p ∈ P(c_i)}, CI(c_i))

Three-valued: verified, failed, or tainted.

Definition 3 — Auto-Remediation Bound. When a verification check fails (V(c) = False), autonomous remediation agents trigger corrective patches. The lower bound of successful auto-remediation probability is formalized as:

P(Remediation) ≥ 1 − (λ_fail/μ_remediate)^K · e^(−γ·T_react)

Where λ_fail is the rate of post-merge integration failures; μ_remediate is the processing capacity of remediation agents; K is the active agent capacity tier depth; T_react is the remediation time budget; and γ is a decay coefficient.

Proposition 3 — Minimum Tier Depth. If ρ < 1, the right-hand side of Equation 3 is non-decreasing in K and in T_react, and tends to 1 as K grows without bound. For a target residual risk ε ∈ (0, 1), the bound is at least 1 − ε whenever:

K ≥ (ln(1/ε) − γ·T_react) / ln(1/ρ)

Verified-Head Discipline. The principal risk of lock-free entry is that the trunk head may be temporarily unverified. AetherFlow confines that risk by distinguishing two references:

  • Admission head — advances on every append.
  • Verified head — the most recent changeset all of whose ancestors are verified.

Producers branch from, and agents read context from, the admission head so that they see the latest work. Anything that requires a known-good state — releases, deployments, downstream consumers, human review views — reads the verified head.

Scope & Limits

What AetherFlow supports:

  • Lock-free merge entry.
  • Post-merge verification (three-valued).
  • Proactive remediation with guardrails.
  • Verified-head discipline.
  • Open manuscript (CC-BY-4.0) and open code (MIT).

What AetherFlow does not support:

  • Empirical benchmarking against real merge queues (not executed).
  • Causal claims (formal analysis only).
  • Human evaluation.

Design philosophy. AetherFlow is a research paper, not a predictive model or a policy tool. Its purpose is to specify an architecture precise enough to be tested against actual agentic workloads — not to make forecasts or normative claims.
